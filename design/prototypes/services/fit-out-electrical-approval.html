Plain-language answer

A DEWA fit-out electrical approval is the authority's review of the electrical design for a commercial unit before supply is released to the fitted-out space. It checks the load schedule, single-line diagram, metering and compliance with the approved supply. It is not the building permit or the Civil Defence NOC.

What this approval is

A fit-out changes the electrical reality of a unit. New lighting, air-conditioning, kitchen equipment, medical devices or IT loads change the current that the supply has to carry. The fit-out electrical approval is the point where the authority checks that the proposed distribution can be connected safely to the approved supply, and that the metering and protection arrangement matches the property.

It is a design-stage approval. It does not certify that the installation has been built correctly; that is the role of the LV inspection and the test pack. It also does not replace the landlord's consent, the building permit, the Civil Defence review or any free-zone approval that applies to the unit. Those are separate files with separate owners.

The practical value of getting the design review right is that the site can be built to an approved drawing. When the drawing and the installation agree, the inspection is a verification exercise. When they disagree, the project carries a second review cycle, a site variation and a delay that no amount of chasing can recover.

What the authority is checking

DEWA's review is not a general opinion on the design. It is a check against the approved supply, the applicable regulations and the information in the application. The exact checklist depends on the service and the property, but a fit-out package normally has to make these points clear:

  • Point of supply and metering. Where the supply enters, how it is metered, whether the meter is whole-current or CT-operated, and whether the meter position is accessible and protected.
  • Connected load and maximum demand. The installed equipment, the demand factors used, the resulting maximum demand and the spare capacity left for the unit.
  • Distribution. Main board, sub-main boards, final circuits, protective devices, cable sizes and the coordination between them.
  • Earthing and bonding. The earth path, main protective bonding, equipotential bonding and the ratings that support the design.
  • Single-line relationship. The SLD must match the load schedule and the equipment list; a mismatch is one of the most common reasons a package comes back.
  • Special loads. Kitchen equipment, lifts, pumps, medical imaging, UPS, generators, EV chargers and solar inverters need to be shown with their own characteristics and protection.

The review is evidence-led. A drawing that says â€œas per siteâ€ or a schedule with unexplained diversity is harder to approve than a drawing that names the equipment, the calculation and the assumption behind each number.

Who needs it, and when it is not needed

Most commercial fit-outs need an electrical review before the unit can draw power under the new arrangement. That includes offices, retail units, restaurants and cafÃ©s, clinics, salons, gyms, showrooms and light industrial units. The trigger is not the size of the tenancy; it is whether the electrical design, load or supply arrangement changes.

It is also needed when a tenant takes a shell-and-core unit and builds the internal distribution, when an existing unit changes activity, and when a landlord's base-build supply is being extended to a new tenant. A fit-out that only replaces finishes, with no change to circuits, equipment or load, may still need landlord and building-permit sign-off, but it may not need a new electrical design review. That distinction should be confirmed against the approved drawings, not assumed from the scope description.

When it is not the right first step

If the proposed equipment exceeds the approved capacity, the first technical question is the load upgrade, not the fit-out approval. If there is no supply at all, the new connection is the parent file. If the work is only a like-for-like meter replacement, the meter or inspection service may be the correct route. Sending the wrong application first creates a paper trail that is difficult to unwind.

Preconditions

Before a fit-out package is worth submitting, four things should be true. The tenancy and landlord consent should be in place. The unit's existing supply and premise number should be known. The proposed equipment should be selected, not guessed. And the design should be coordinated with the architectural and MEP layouts so the electrical drawings do not describe a different room.

Document checklist

The exact transmittal depends on the property and the service. A fit-out package normally includes the following. Each document should carry a title, number, revision and date, and the transmittal should match the file names.

  • Tenancy contract and trade licence for the applicant.
  • Landlord or developer NOC and the building's fit-out rules.
  • Authorised signatory letter, if the applicant is not the licence holder.
  • Premise or customer number and the existing supply details.
  • Architectural layout and the electrical equipment schedule.
  • Connected-load schedule with demand factors and maximum demand.
  • Single-line diagram from the point of supply to the final boards.
  • Metering arrangement, including CT ratio where applicable.
  • Earthing and bonding details, with protective device ratings.
  • Kitchen, medical, UPS, generator, EV or solar details where present.
  • Inspection and test plan for the completed installation.

Timing

Authority review time is not a fixed number. It moves with the completeness of the package, the number of review cycles, the availability of the authority's engineers and the complexity of the supply. A package with a clean SLD, a reconciled load schedule and a landlord consent usually moves faster than a package that needs a second explanation.

Range withheld
This design prototype does not publish a timing range. The production sheet will show an observed range from DEWA Approvals Dubai's files, with the sample size, the conditions and the review date. Until that evidence exists, the honest answer is that the range is not yet verified.

The programme should be built backwards from the opening date: inspection slot, defect close-out, meter release, construction, authority review, design. If the fit-out contractor is appointed late, the electrical review becomes the critical path. That is a programme decision, not an authority delay.

Cost drivers, not invented prices

Government fees and consultant fees are different things. The authority fee depends on the service and the property; the consultant fee depends on the work needed to produce a submittable package. Neither is a single market price. The drivers below are the ones that change the effort or the authority charge:

  • The number of boards, circuits and specialist systems in the unit.
  • Whether the supply is whole-current or CT-operated, and whether the meter position changes.
  • Whether the existing approved load is adequate or a load upgrade is required.
  • The quality of the existing drawings and the landlord's document set.
  • Kitchen, medical, UPS, generator, EV or solar equipment that needs its own design evidence.
  • The number of review cycles and the site inspections required.
  • Whether the work is in a free zone or a master community with additional approvals.

A fixed-scope quotation should list what is included, what is excluded, which authority fees are pass-through and which deposits are refundable. It should not hide a second submission in a low headline fee.

Inspection notes

The fit-out approval is a design decision; the inspection is where the site proves it. The installation should match the approved SLD, the load schedule and the equipment actually installed. Boards should be labelled, circuits should be identified, and the meter position should be accessible without dismantling finishes. The test pack should include insulation resistance, earth-loop impedance and RCD tests, with the tester, date and instrument details.

If the equipment changed during construction, do not wait for the inspector to discover it. Issue a revised design and confirm whether the change needs a formal amendment. A small equipment substitution can be a minor revision; a new kitchen line or a larger chiller can be a different load case. The difference matters.

Fit-out approval questions

01Does a shell-and-core unit need fit-out approval before the contractor starts?

The electrical design should be reviewed before the installation is built, not after. The contractor can mobilise for preliminaries and non-electrical work, but the distribution, containment and equipment should follow an approved design. Starting the electrical installation on an unapproved layout creates a high risk of rework and a difficult inspection.

02Can the fit-out approval use the landlord's existing SLD?

The landlord's SLD is evidence for the base-build supply, not a substitute for the tenant's fit-out design. The submission should show how the unit connects to the approved supply, what changes at the metering point, and how the new boards and circuits are protected. A reference to the base-build drawing is useful, but it does not replace the unit-level design.

Draft / source verification pendingDEWA source
03What load schedule detail causes the most comments?

Unexplained diversity. A schedule that lists equipment and then applies a demand factor without showing why is difficult to review. The fix is to state the equipment, the connected load, the demand factor and the resulting maximum demand for each group, then show how the total relates to the supply and the main protective device. The reviewer should be able to follow the arithmetic without a separate note.

04Is a DEWA fit-out approval the same as a building permit?

No. The building permit is the construction approval from the building authority; the DEWA fit-out approval is the electricity and water review of the unit's design. They can run in parallel, and one may depend on the other, but they are separate files with separate references. A fit-out should not treat one approval as evidence that the other has been granted.

Draft / source verification pendingDEWA source
05Can the approval be transferred if the tenant changes?

An approval is tied to the application, the premise and the design that was reviewed. A new tenant with a different activity, load or fit-out should not assume the old approval transfers automatically. The safe route is to check the approval's scope and validity, then confirm with the authority whether an amendment, a new application or a transfer is required. The answer depends on what actually changes.

06What if the equipment changes after approval?

Compare the change with the approved load case. A like-for-like substitution within the same rating may be a minor revision; a larger chiller, an additional kitchen line or a new imaging unit can change the maximum demand and the protection. Issue a revised drawing and load schedule, and confirm whether the authority requires a formal amendment before the inspection.
