Fit-Out Compliance Guide

The Complete Guide to DEWA Fit-Out NOC in Dubai (2026 Edition)

Published by Senior Electrical Liaison Division • 6 min read

Securing a DEWA No Objection Certificate (NOC) is the mandatory gateway before any commercial interior decoration, retail transformation, or office layout alterations can proceed in Dubai. Without this certificate, building management will not release work permits, and contractors face substantial municipality and utility fines.

Stage 1: Tenancy & Landlord Verification

The process initiates with confirming the unit's active Premise ID and reviewing the building's tenancy handbook. Malls such as Dubai Mall, Mall of the Emirates, or Nakheel properties enforce specific spatial constraints, isolating switchgear rules, and emergency lighting interfaces that must reflect in your electrical schematics.

Stage 2: Single-Line Diagram (SLD) Drafting

Under Dubai law, generic interior draftsmen cannot submit electrical drawings. Only certified electrical contractors holding active DEWA licenses can stamp and submit the official SLD. The drawing must calculate diversified maximum demand, specify cable fire-retardant classes (e.g. FP200 for life safety circuits), and confirm breaker grading.

Stage 3: DEWA E-Services Portal Submission

The contractor uploads the drawings, Ejari contract, landlord approval letter, and equipment schedule through the official DEWA business portal. Review engineers examine cable capacity, voltage drop equations (<4%), and breaker fault ratings. With Abbadi Tech, typical review clearance is achieved within 3 to 5 business days.

Power Engineering

How to Upgrade Electrical Load for Commercial Kitchens & Bakeries

Published by Lead Power Systems Specialist • 7 min read

When a commercial unit originally designed for dry retail (e.g. 25 kW capacity) is converted into a high-volume restaurant, heavy cooking equipment, deck ovens, blast chillers, and exhaust hoods demand between 80 kW to 200+ kW. Attempting to operate on the existing breaker causes constant tripping and utility penalties.

Understanding Transformer Headroom vs Unit Allocation

An electrical load upgrade requires a two-level feasibility check: (1) Does the building's main substation transformer have remaining kVA capacity? (2) Does the existing incoming cable from the electrical riser support the enhanced amperage without hazardous overheating?

When Current Transformer (CT) Chambers Become Mandatory

In Dubai, when a premise's connected load requires an incomer circuit breaker exceeding 100 Amps three-phase, direct whole-current metering is no longer permissible by DEWA. The installation must incorporate an authority-approved Current Transformer (CT) metering chamber with certified test blocks and spatial clearances.

Inspection Troubleshooting

Top 7 Reasons DEWA Rejects Electrical Inspections & How to Fix Them

Published by Site Compliance Division • 5 min read

A failed DEWA inspection results in an official "Disapproval Notice" (Red Notice), halting your handover, delaying your launch date, and causing expensive rent losses. Here are the most frequent violations identified by DEWA field inspectors:

  1. Earth Pit Resistance Exceeding 1.0 Ohm: DEWA strictly mandates earthing loop resistance below 1.0Ω. Soil drying or improper copper rod bonding frequently causes rejection.
  2. Mismatched Cable Sizing: Pulling undersized cables that do not match the approved Single-Line Diagram breaker trip rating.
  3. Phase Imbalance Across Red, Yellow & Blue: Uneven distribution of single-phase loads causing high neutral currents and phase deviation over 10%.
  4. Missing or Incorrect RCD Sensitivities: Sockets must have 30mA RCD protection; machinery and HVAC require 100mA or 300mA time-delayed units.
  5. Unferruled Wiring & Missing DB Directories: Every wire must feature clear ferrule numbering corresponding precisely to the laminated circuit chart inside the DB door.
  6. Inadequate Space Clearances around Meter Cubicles: Obstructed access or failure to provide minimum 1000mm front clearance in front of panels.
  7. Failure to Perform Pre-Testing with Calibrated Meters: Submitting for inspection without prior insulation resistance (Megger) certification.
